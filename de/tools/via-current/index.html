You entered several vias: the total is simply one via × count. We do not give you a parallel derating factor — pad cooling, copper connections and via spacing change the answer, and only a thermal measurement or simulation settles it.
Plating below 15 µm: typical fab electroplated barrels land in the 18-25 µm range. Under that, barrel resistance rises and current capacity falls, and the result depends heavily on process capability — treat this as a sensitivity check rather than a conclusion.
Temperature rise is 0 or negative: the fit yields 0 A at ΔT=0, so nothing can be inverted. Set an allowed rise of at least 1 °C.
Drill or plating is 0 / negative: the barrel geometry does not exist, so no result. Mechanical drills start around 0.3 mm; laser microvias can reach 0.1 mm but with much thinner plating — do not swap the two numbers.

Where the number comes from

A = π · (d + t) · t I = k · ΔT^0.44 · A^0.725 R = ρ · L / A
  • d: drill diameter before plating (mil)
  • t: barrel plating thickness (mil); 1 oz = 1.378 mil, 1 µm = 0.03937 mil
  • k: 0.048 (outer) / 0.024 (internal), ΔT being the allowed rise
  • ρ: annealed copper resistivity at 20 °C = 0.0172 Ω·mm²/m, with L taken as the board thickness

The fit reproduces the published IPC-2221 current capacity chart; it is not the standard text. All constants are listed below, please check them.

Constants: k=0.048 (outer layer) / 0.024 (internal layer) · b=0.44 · c=0.725 · 1 oz=1.378 mil=35 µm · 1 mil=0.0254 mm=25.4 µm · rho(Cu, 20 °C)=0.0172 ohm·mm²/m

What this result cannot be used to decide

This is via geometry × a temperature-rise model. It does not cover the plating uniformity declared by the fab or aspect-ratio capability, thermal-cycle and reflow reliability or the solder fill inside the hole, and it cannot know how current actually splits between several vias. Production sign-off needs a fab process confirmation and a measured temperature rise.

Frequently asked questions

How much current can one via really carry?

The dominant term is the plated barrel cross-section, A = π·(d+t)·t — so hole size and plating thickness, not the drill diameter alone. A 0.3 mm via with 25 µm plating, outer layer, 10 °C rise comes out around 1.9 A.

Can I multiply the current by paralleling vias?

Arithmetically yes, and the page reports one via × count as that sum — but do not treat it as a guaranteed value. How current splits depends on the pads and copper paths, and closely spaced vias heat each other. We deliberately do not print a "×0.7 derating" number, because that only holds once you have your own thermal boundary conditions.

What plating thickness should I enter?

Typical fab electroplated barrels land around 18-25 µm, so 25 µm is the common assumption; once you have the fab cross-section report, use the measured value. Below 15 µm the page flags that you have left the normal band.

Is the barrel resistance useful?

Negligible for small-signal paths (milliohms), but worth counting above about 1 A, or when doing current sensing and voltage-drop budgets. It is computed as R = ρL/A with L equal to the board thickness, which is a conservative upper bound since real plating is more resistive than bulk copper.

Why is the internal-layer figure lower?

The same fit with k = 0.024 for internal layers: heat stays trapped in the dielectric. In real boards, whether the via lands on a large pad or thick copper often changes the answer more than that coefficient does.
