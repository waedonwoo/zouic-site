The 300 Million Dollar Order That Is Not Revenue

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

After Agility Robotics completed its merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI, a group of figures was widely quoted: about 465 million dollars in financing, more than 300 million dollars in Digit 5 committed orders, more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation and a RoboFab design annual capacity of up to 10,000 units. These four figures are different kinds of framing and cannot be added together or substituted for one another. The 300 million dollars carries contractual milestone, product functionality and specification conditions and does not represent current revenue.

Four figures and what they point to

The 4 November 2026 announcement said the merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI was complete and that the stock and warrants would trade on Nasdaq from 5 November under AGLT and AGLTW. The 5 November announcement gave total merger and related financing of about 465 million dollars before taxes and fees, together with more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation and more than 300 million dollars in multi-year Digit 5 committed orders, the latter subject to contractual milestones, product functionality and specifications. The 30 October announcement further stated that the 300 million dollars is a potential multi-year value realised over time and does not represent current revenue.

Committed orders are not revenue

The 300 million dollars is not revenue because of its conditions. The 30 October announcement states that this figure is a potential multi-year value realised over time, subject to contractual milestones, product functionality and specifications. Whether customers place orders against the milestones and whether functionality and specifications are met are both undecided. Financing is money received or committed, while committed orders are conditional future value; the two differ in nature. Putting them in the same revenue table would turn unrealised potential value into a realised operating result.

Who provides these figures

The figures all come from Agility Robotics official press releases, checked against the original company text as of 21 November 2026. GXO has moved more than 100,000 totes at its Georgia site and plans to deploy at two further US facilities in early 2027; AGCO plans a pilot at its Kansas plant in 2027; three other manufacturers are in paid programmes; and there are more than 30 active prospects. These are the progress of customers and prospects and belong to different entities and stages from financing and order figures. The NVIDIA collaboration plans IGX Thor as the onboard computing standard and an expansion of Halos safety integration, FORT Robotics handles safety systems and Nyobolt supplies fast-charging technology; the roles differ.

From launch to supply

Digit 5 was released on 15 September 2026; the 30 October announcement defined the framing of the 300 million dollars; the merger completed on 4 November; and the 5 November announcement gave financing and operating figures. Early customer use is expected in the first half of 2027 and formal supply is targeted for the end of 2027. The additional GXO facilities are planned for early 2027 and the AGCO pilot for 2027. The as-of date of 21 November 2026 is when the site checked the reporting, not the date of any of these events. Laid out in time, the multi-year value behind the 300 million dollars extends through 2027 and beyond, a different time scale from current revenue.

Which documents and sites carry the numbers

The financing composition and the order framing sit in the official press releases: about 164 million dollars from the Churchill trust account, about 201 million dollars of common stock PIPE led by Foxconn at 10 dollars per share, and 100 million dollars of equity funding previously received, roughly 465 million dollars in total. Operating hours and tote counts sit in the GXO Georgia site. The 70,000 square foot RoboFab plant and its design annual capacity of up to 10,000 units sit in plant material. The nine-minute charge and more than 20 hours of effective work within 24 hours sit in the Nyobolt collaboration description and are design targets. The documents and contexts behind the numbers differ, which is why they cannot be flattened into one comparison.

Read four kinds of framing separately

Read the group of figures in four steps, by framing. First, the financing amount: about 465 million dollars is the total merger and related financing, made up of trust account funds, a PIPE commitment and 100 million dollars already received, so the receipt times differ. Second, the committed orders: more than 300 million dollars carries contractual milestone, product functionality and specification conditions and is a potential multi-year value. Third, the operating hours: more than 65,000 hours of real-world operation show that real use is accumulating, not revenue or capacity. Fourth, the design annual capacity: up to 10,000 units is the RoboFab design ceiling, not achieved output. Keep the four steps in place and design values, committed values and realised values will not collapse into one account.

Design targets are not field records

Several boundaries need to be kept. The nine-minute charge and more than 20 hours of effective work within 24 hours are design targets of the Nyobolt fast-charging technology, not field records. The design annual capacity of up to 10,000 units is a plant design ceiling, not actual output. The more than 65,000 hours are cumulative real-world operation, not single-unit continuous runtime. The 300 million dollars is a committed order, subject to contractual milestones, not revenue already received. Writing design targets as achieved metrics is the most likely distortion in this kind of data review.

Put each number in its own framing

The figures disclosed by Agility Robotics each have a place: about 465 million dollars is total financing, more than 300 million dollars is a conditional multi-year committed order, more than 65,000 hours is cumulative real-world operation, and up to 10,000 units is design annual capacity. The 300 million dollars does not represent current revenue, and the nine-minute charge and 20 hours of effective work are design targets. Read by framing, progress and expectation each stay in place; added together or written as achieved results, they lose their conditions and the line between design and measurement.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 300 million dollars already received revenue?

No. The 30 October 2026 announcement says this figure is a potential multi-year value realised over time, subject to contractual milestones, product functionality and specifications, and does not represent current revenue. It is a committed order, a different kind of framing from financing, operating hours and design annual capacity.

What do 65,000 hours show, and how do early customers differ from formal supply?

The more than 65,000 hours are cumulative real-world operating hours, showing that real use is accumulating; they are not revenue or single-unit continuous runtime. Early customer use is expected in the first half of 2027 and formal supply is targeted for the end of 2027, two different stages of trial and volume delivery. The additional GXO facilities in 2027 and the AGCO pilot in 2027 fall within this window.
