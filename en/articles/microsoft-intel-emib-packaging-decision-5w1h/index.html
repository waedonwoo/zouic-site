Microsoft Brings in EMIB: First Separate Five Things

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

After Microsoft and Intel’s long-term agreement was published on 18 November 2026, the conclusion most easily written in one sentence was that Microsoft’s in-house chips had shifted to Intel packaging. What the announcement actually says is that Microsoft became the first company to publicly announce the use of Intel Foundry EMIB advanced packaging in its Azure in-house chips, that the two sides confirmed a multi-year Xeon supply extending to the next-generation server CPU codenamed Diamond Rapids, and that the cooperation also covers CXL memory, data security and compression acceleration, and confidential computing. The announcement gives two performance figures, up to 2.3x performance for Azure M-series v4 virtual machines and up to 2.5x speed for cloud SQL backups, both with specific configuration premises. The announcement does not disclose the contract value, purchase volumes, the specific chip models using EMIB, the packaging order size or the mass-production time. Customer endorsement, the scope of the agreement, the performance figures and the undisclosed items belong to different layers; compressing them into one sentence makes a judgement lose its premises.

What the announcement says

According to Intel’s official news page, the two sides announced a long-term agreement expanding a partnership that has lasted decades, to advance several areas of Azure infrastructure together. There are four core points. First, Microsoft is the first company to announce the use of Intel Foundry EMIB embedded multi-die interconnect bridge advanced packaging for Azure in-house chips. Second, the agreement confirms a multi-year supply of Xeon processors, including next-generation server CPU instances codenamed Diamond Rapids. Third, the two sides will work on Xeon processors with CXL to improve memory efficiency across Azure. Fourth, the two sides will deepen cooperation on Intel data protection and compression acceleration technology and on confidential computing. These are terms of the agreement, not specifications of already-delivered products; when citing them, state the source and the as-of date.

Five things cannot be merged into one conclusion

Being the first to publicly announce adoption means a public endorsement relationship exists, not that the packaging order size is already clear. A multi-year Xeon supply means the supply relationship continues, not that the next-generation product is already purchasable. The three directions of CXL, QAT and TDX indicate the scope of technical cooperation, and the performance figure in each direction carries a specific configuration premise. The 2.3x performance appears on Azure M-series v4 virtual machines using CXL memory and Xeon 6 processors in flat memory mode, and the 2.5x backup speed appears on the first cloud virtual machines to support QAT; the two target different workloads and cannot be used for a unified comparison or extrapolated to all Azure workloads. The five things answer respectively about endorsement, supply, memory, acceleration and security, and belong to different layers. Skipping these and writing directly that Microsoft has shifted to Intel packaging compares information under different framings and yields no actionable judgement.

Who announces, who relays, who verifies

The releasing parties are Intel and Microsoft; the official announcement appears on Intel’s news page and is tier 2 company-official framing. Cailian Press, Zhongxin Jingwei and International Electronic Business relayed the announcement on 19 November 2026, and relaying does not change the source tier. The announcement quotes statements by Saurabh Dighe, Vice President of Azure Systems and Architecture at Microsoft, and Naga Chandrasekaran, Executive Vice President of Intel Foundry, both describing cooperation intent and directions for joint optimisation, which is company framing. The media recollection that Microsoft’s existing in-house chips use TSMC processes and CoWoS-S packaging is compiled information and does not enter confirmed conclusions. The purchasing side must organise its own verification team: the architecture team checks specific models and packaging schemes, the supply chain checks capacity and milestones, and the performance team re-tests under target workloads. The announcement provides intent, the media provides propagation, and the verifier provides measurement; the three cannot substitute for one another.

The announcement, milestones and undisclosed times

Time points must be recorded separately. 18 November 2026 is the announcement date, and 19 November is the media relay date. The announcement states that the multi-year supply relationship extends to Diamond Rapids, but gives no market-launch time for this generation. The announcement likewise gives no specific chip model using EMIB, no mass-production time and no packaging-order execution milestone. The as-of date of 23 November 2026 is this site’s checking point, not the time of the announcement or of product launch. Treating the announcement date as the product availability date is a common bias in this kind of agreement news. Missing dates must be left blank, not filled with an approximate time.

Which layer of material each statement sits in

Which layer of material each statement sits in determines how far it can be used. Microsoft being the first to adopt EMIB, the multi-year Xeon supply, Diamond Rapids being included in the cooperation, and the three directions of CXL, QAT and TDX sit in the two sides’ official announcement; the two figures 2.3x and 2.5x sit in the announcement’s description of specific virtual-machine configurations; the recollection of Microsoft’s existing Maia and Cobalt products’ process and packaging sits in the media-compilation layer; and speculation about order size and mass-production time sits in the third-party judgement layer. When citing, keep the source and the configuration premise in the same sentence, and do not mix up the announcement terms and the media compilation. Different layers of material answer different questions; mixing them makes a conclusion lose its boundaries.

To read this kind of agreement, ask five things first

Reading this kind of agreement can proceed through five questions. First, ask about the scope of the endorsement: being the first to publicly announce adoption, which product and which generation, and does the announcement name them. Second, ask about the supply terms: which instances the multi-year Xeon supply covers, and when Diamond Rapids instances become available. Third, ask about the performance framing: what configuration, what workload and what comparison baseline each of 2.3x and 2.5x corresponds to, and whether it can be reproduced on your own workloads. Fourth, ask about the packaging division of labour: which chips use EMIB, whether existing orders are being transferred, and who bears capacity and yield. Fifth, ask about the undisclosed items: the contract value, purchase volumes and mass-production time do not appear in the announcement, so do not add them to the conclusion. Leave a source for all five, and the judgement has a common baseline.

Public endorsement and performance figures do not enter confirmed orders

Several boundaries must be held. Being the first to publicly announce adoption does not mean the packaging order size is clear; the announcement names no specific chip model and does not say whether existing orders are being transferred. A multi-year Xeon supply is a term of the agreement, not proof that the next-generation product is already purchasable. The 2.3x performance appears under a specific virtual-machine configuration and the 2.5x backup speed on another type of virtual machine; the two are not a comparison of the same workload and do not constitute a general performance advantage. The contract value, purchase volumes, mass-production time and capacity scale are all undisclosed, so specific figures in these directions lie outside the verified information. The media compilation of existing products’ packaging roadmaps can only serve as directional reference. Once the limiting conditions are dropped, an agreement announcement is read as an already-landed order; this is the most common way agreement news becomes distorted.

Keep separate accounts for the five things

What can be confirmed: Microsoft and Intel have a long-term agreement, Microsoft is the first to publicly announce the use of Intel Foundry EMIB in Azure in-house chips, the multi-year Xeon supply extends to Diamond Rapids, the cooperation covers CXL, QAT and TDX, and the announcement gives two performance figures with configuration premises. What cannot be confirmed: the contract value, purchase volumes, the specific chip models using EMIB, the packaging order size, the mass-production time, and whether the two performance figures can be reproduced on our own workloads. The judgement order is to ask about the endorsement scope first, then the supply terms, then check the performance framing and packaging division of labour, and finally leave the undisclosed items blank. This order applies to any cooperation announcement between a cloud provider and a foundry. Keeping separate accounts for the five things is closer to being actionable than memorising eye-catching phrases such as 2.3x or EMIB.

Frequently asked questions

Has Microsoft switched all its in-house chips to EMIB?

There is no evidence for that conclusion. The official announcement says Microsoft is the first company to publicly announce the use of Intel Foundry EMIB technology in its Azure in-house chips, but it names no specific chip model, says nothing about whether existing packaging orders are being transferred, and gives no mass-production time. Being the first to publicly announce adoption means a public endorsement exists; it does not mean all in-house chips have switched, nor that the packaging order size is clear.

Can 2.3x and 2.5x be compared directly?

No. The 2.3x performance appears on Azure M-series v4 virtual machines using CXL memory and Xeon 6 processors in flat memory mode, while the 2.5x backup speed appears on the first cloud virtual machines to support Intel QAT; the two correspond to different workloads, different configurations and different comparison baselines, and are scenario-specific figures given separately in the announcement. Putting them in one table to rank them, or treating them as a general performance advantage, goes beyond what the announcement can support.

Can Diamond Rapids be purchased now?

The announcement gives no availability time. The agreement confirms a multi-year Xeon supply extending to the next-generation server CPU codenamed Diamond Rapids, meaning the supply relationship covers this generation, but the announcement gives no launch time, specific instance specifications or price. Supply terms and product availability are two different things; a purchasing decision should wait until the instances are officially online with verifiable specifications.
