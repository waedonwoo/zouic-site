An interface standard is slowly taking apart the CoWoS premium

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

For two years the advanced-packaging conversation has circled CoWoS capacity. But tight capacity is only the surface: what decides who can charge a premium is how much it costs a customer to switch packaging houses. Once open die-to-die specifications such as UCIe fix the interface, that cost starts falling - and packaging drifts from a scarce resource back toward an engineering service you can put out to bid.

First, admit the physics really did get harder

A Broadcom executive gave a hard judgement in a 2026 industry analysis: beyond roughly 5,000 mm² for a single AI die, 2.5D/3D packaging is the only viable path; soaring current density forces the power delivery network to be redesigned from chip level to board level; cooling jumps from air to liquid or immersion; and ABF substrates, glass cores and 800 V DC must iterate in lockstep, with the lagging link becoming the system's weakest point.

That 'second Moore's Law' framing has a direct consequence: the tighter the coupling between packaging, power and cooling, the harder it is for a customer to change just one of them. Add hybrid-bonding and inspection equipment now sitting on export-control lists and CoWoS capacity concentrated at TSMC, and geopolitical risk is priced straight into the capacity premium. Third-party estimates also put HPC at 66% of TSMC revenue in Q2 2026, up from about 31% in 2020, while smartphones fell from 48% to 22%; in 2025 NVIDIA overtook Apple as TSMC's largest customer (about 19% versus 17%, analyst estimate). The more concentrated the demand, the stronger the pricing power of a single supply step.

How a standard takes the premium apart

UCIe does something unglamorous but decisive: it writes the physical layer, protocol and test conventions between chiplets into a public specification. Once the die-to-die interface is no longer privately defined by one packaging house, a customer can hand compute dies, IO dies and HBM base dies to different suppliers and compare prices at integration. The same analysis names Intel EMIB and Samsung I-Cube as credible alternatives and says UCIe standardisation is eroding any single packaging vendor's pricing power.

That does not make CoWoS worthless overnight. Capacity, yield and jointly validated process combinations are still scarce, particularly while glass cores, backside power and 800 V all have to change together. What gets dismantled is lock-in, not experience: tomorrow's premium will go to yield and co-debug capability rather than to being the only shop that can take the job.

The competitive axis has moved

The same 12-24 month outlook gives a sequence: liquid cooling becomes the default, glass substrates enter production validation, and 800 V architectures land in hyperscale data centres; the axis shifts from who owns the leading node to who integrates the system most efficiently. That lines up with what earlier notes observed - 30x throughput on 3% more HBM, eight spare GPUs in every rack, power moved to the wafer backside - all of them squeezing efficiency at system level rather than waiting for the next lithography step.

For procurement and investment the implication is concrete: seeing a vendor win the most advanced node no longer implies pricing power. Follow up by asking whether its interconnect standard is open, whether chiplets can change supplier, and whether power and cooling can be decoupled. Anything that can be sold as separate pieces will eventually be priced as separate pieces.

Bottom line

The advanced-packaging shortage is real, but the excess profit it creates will not last forever. Standards like UCIe turn interfaces from private assets into public infrastructure, while EMIB, I-Cube and future glass-panel options fill out the choice set. What can still be charged for over the long run is yield, co-debugging and delivery certainty. Watch substitutability, not just capacity.

Frequently asked questions

Will UCIe make CoWoS cheaper right away?

No. Capacity, yield and jointly validated process combinations remain scarce, especially with glass cores, backside power and 800 V all changing together. Standardisation lowers the cost of switching suppliers; the premium shifts from being the only option to having the best yield.

Why must packaging, power and cooling change together?

Beyond roughly 5,000 mm² for one AI die, 2.5D/3D is the only path. Rising current density forces the power network to be redesigned from chip level to board level, and heat flux makes liquid cooling mandatory. The three are coupled - changing one affects the other two.

Are EMIB and I-Cube credible alternatives now?

A Broadcom executive named Intel EMIB and Samsung I-Cube as credible alternatives in a 2026 industry analysis and noted that UCIe standardisation is eroding any single packaging vendor’s pricing power. High-volume production track record, however, still sits mainly with TSMC CoWoS.
