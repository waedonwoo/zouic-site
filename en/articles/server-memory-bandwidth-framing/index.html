Server Memory Bandwidth: Separate the Medium Before Ranking the Numbers

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

Server configuration sheets often place several bandwidth numbers side by side: LPDDR6 is 45% faster than the previous generation, HBM4 is about 2 TB/s, and CXL pooling moves memory into a shared pool. They all appear to answer how much faster, but they are not measuring the same thing. Sorting them in one table by size gives procurement a tidy conclusion that cannot be used. More importantly, the biggest number tends to become the purchasing target even when its carrier and workload are outside the scope of the purchase.

What each of the three numbers actually says

Samsung used the November 2026 CIIE to position LPDDR6 as the next-generation low-power DRAM for AI scenarios, with transfer bandwidth 45% above the previous generation. JEDEC JESD270-4 specifies HBM4 with a 2048-bit interface at 8 Gb/s, about 2 TB/s in total; this article uses that figure only as a framing reference and does not unpack HBM4 details. SK hynix showed three module tiers: a 256GB high-capacity module, a 128GB high-bandwidth module and a 192GB module balancing power efficiency and rack space. Micron brought the SOCAMM2 low-power high-performance module and the low latency of the 7600 SSD. The 45% is a generational ratio, 2 TB/s is an interface peak, and module capacity is a loading amount; the three statements each talk about a different thing. For procurement they correspond to the gain from a generation change, the physical ceiling of an interface and the choice of deployment form, so the questions asked and the answers obtained differ.

Different denominators mean no ranking

The denominator of 45% is the transfer bandwidth of the previous LPDDR generation, answering how much faster a generation change makes things. The denominator of about 2 TB/s is 2048-bit multiplied by 8 Gb/s, answering how many bits that interface can move per second. CXL CMM-D pooling answers whether memory can be moved out of a single server and allocated on demand from a resource pool. One is a relative value of medium iteration, one is an absolute value of interface design, and one is an allocation method brought by architecture. Ranking them first and second is like putting speed, engine displacement and seat count in the same column. Different denominators, units and questions make ranking meaningless; keeping separate columns is what works. Mixing relative and absolute values also lets a high ratio on a small base drown out a limited increment on a large base, which looks dramatic on paper but adds little usable bandwidth on the machine.

Who supplied the numbers and in what capacity

Vendor claims came from the November 2026 CIIE floor: Samsung on the 45% of LPDDR6, SK hynix on the three capacity tiers, Micron on SOCAMM2 and the 7600 SSD. The HBM4 figure comes from the JEDEC JESD270-4 specification text, tier 1, and is borrowed here only to show how an interface peak is calculated. The claims about CXL CMM-D and the SK MARS architecture come from vendor and demonstration material; SK says first-token latency can drop by 3.5 to 5.2 times, tier 4, used only as a directional reference. Who said a number and in what capacity should be written on the same line as the number; once promotional values, specification figures and demonstration claims are mixed, their sources can no longer be traced. Keeping the tier next to the figure also prevents demonstration material from being read as specification clauses or vendor targets as delivered capability.

Release points and the as-of date

CIIE took place in November 2026, and what vendors gave at their booths were product positioning and promotional values. The JESD270-4 specification text predates the show, and the as-of date of 22 November 2026 is only the checking point for this article, not the publication date of the specification or the opening day of the show. SK MARS first-token latency comes from demonstration claims, and the same material does not give the full conditions, model scale or comparison baseline, so it cannot be treated as reproducible on the as-of date. Clear time labels tell you what is available to buy now and what is still planned; writing plans as available stock is the most common source of error in a configuration sheet. Show claims describe current positioning, specification text describes an achievable interface target, and demonstration claims describe a result under specific conditions; their time attributes differ.

Which layer of carrier the numbers land on

Carrier form is the first question. Onboard components solder LPDDR6 to the motherboard, so the bandwidth gain travels with the board; adding memory later means replacing the board, and so does repair. DIMMs are the three tiers SK hynix showed: 256GB for capacity, 128GB for bandwidth and 192GB for power efficiency and rack space, with insertion and replacement handled at the rack. SOCAMM2 is Micron low-power high-performance module following another packaging path, so the replacement flow for ordinary modules does not carry over. CXL pooling adds a layer of memory resource pool outside the server, accessed remotely through a controller. The carrier decides which machine a number lands on and who handles expansion. Fault domains differ too: a failed onboard part means a board swap, a failed module means a module swap, and a pooling failure first needs to be located as local or remote.

Ask about the carrier, then the workload, then the peak

A sensible procurement sequence has three steps. First ask about the carrier form: onboard components, pluggable modules or pooled memory, which decides whether later expansion is possible, how a failure is replaced and who replaces it. Second look at the workload: large-model inference cares about the match of capacity and bandwidth, databases about latency and persistence, and the edge about power, so the low latency claimed for the 7600 SSD only holds when tied to a specific task; the same memory behaves differently under two workloads, which is only common sense. Only then look at peak figures and ask at what components, channel count and temperature the peak was measured. Reversing the order lets the largest number lead you by the hand, and you discover too late that rack space or power ran out first. Writing the three steps into a request for quotation forces suppliers to state the carrier, workload fit and test conditions instead of quoting a single peak.

Promotional values and specification values are not delivered performance

Three boundaries belong in the evaluation sheet. The 45% is a vendor generational ratio without test conditions or a comparison model, so it cannot be converted into the actual throughput of a given server. About 2 TB/s is the theoretical interface peak of JESD270-4, not a speed any whole machine can sustain; the machine also depends on the memory controller, channel count and cooling. CXL pooling and SK MARS figures are tier 4; vendor and demonstration claims only indicate direction and do not enter a firm conclusion. One more is easy to miss: capacity, bandwidth, power and rack space are four constraints, and choosing on any single one should be avoided; the first wall a project hits is often not bandwidth. Module tiers shown at a show mean the vendor has those product lines, not that a target machine has completed adaptation or entered volume supply.

Put the three framings side by side, not in a queue

Server memory bandwidth figures should be separated into medium, interface and architecture columns rather than ranked by size. The 45% of LPDDR6 belongs to medium iteration, the about 2 TB/s of HBM4 to interface specification and CXL pooling to the architecture layer; they answer different questions, and comparing them side by side yields no clearer conclusion. Start selection with the carrier form, then align the workload, and finally check the test conditions behind the peak figure. Read this way, the table gives procurement a list of conditions that can be checked item by item rather than a ranking that cannot be implemented; the numbers stay the same, only the way they are used changes. Once separated, each figure stays in its own context and the discussion can settle on verifiable conditions.

Frequently asked questions

Can the 45% of LPDDR6 and the 2 TB/s of HBM4 be compared in one table?

No. The 45% is the LPDDR6 transfer bandwidth gain relative to the previous LPDDR generation, a generational difference from medium iteration. About 2 TB/s is the HBM4 interface peak from JESD270-4, calculated as 2048-bit multiplied by 8 Gb/s, an interface design value. One answers how much faster a generation change makes things, the other how many bits the interface moves per second, so the denominators and units differ. Recording each in its own column with its source and conditions is more useful than ranking them first and second.

What should procurement ask first about server memory?

Ask about the carrier form first: onboard components, pluggable modules or pooled memory, which decides whether later expansion is possible, how a failure is replaced and who replaces it. Then look at the workload: large-model inference cares about the match of capacity and bandwidth, databases about latency and persistence, and the edge about power. Only then look at peak figures and ask about the components, channel count and temperature used in the test. Capacity, bandwidth, power and rack space are four parallel constraints, and the first one to run out is often not bandwidth.

Can CXL pooling and SK MARS figures go into a selection conclusion?

Not as a firm conclusion. Claims about CXL CMM-D memory pooling and the SK MARS architecture come from vendor and demonstration material; SK says first-token latency can drop by 3.5 to 5.2 times, which is tier 4 directional information with incomplete conditions, model scale and comparison baseline. It can point to a direction, such as including memory pooling among the options, but cannot be treated as a reproducible figure on the as-of date and does not enter a firm conclusion.
