Compute & Semiconductor Notes

Updated as things change

Racks, power, memory, packaging, software stacks — and inspection checklists, fault triage, BOM arithmetic. The bottleneck is rarely the most expensive chip. These notes are filed by the question they answer, one per piece, with figures traced back to their source; vendor benchmarks and independent results are kept apart. Every article is available in Chinese, English and German.

Racks and buying decisions

1,000 racks a week: the unit of the AI compute race is no longer the chip

NVIDIA says it ships over 1,000 MGX racks a week, while hyperscalers install roughly 1,000 NVL72 racks — about 72,000 Blackwell GPUs. Supply only makes sense once you count racks.

  • B300 vs MI355X: memory capacity against per-chip throughput — In MLPerf v6.0 the MI355X beats the B200 by 19% on interactive serving and 11–15% on GPT-OSS-120B, yet still trails the B300. 288 GB against 192 GB is changing how buyers choose.
  • Six months to fourteen days: DSX Pod sells AI factories by the container — Seven NVL72 racks in one container cut deployment time roughly 13-fold. Time to production is becoming harder to copy than a spec sheet.
  • AMD Helios: from selling chips to selling racks — 6 GW for OpenAI, 50,000 chips for Oracle, 2 GW for Anthropic — contracted watts are not shipped units. Actual 2027 deployments will be the test.
  • Wait for Rubin or buy Blackwell now? A procurement calculation — Rubin is ramping, but the hidden cost of waiting — rent, queue position, idle business — usually exceeds putting the previous generation to work today.
  • CloudMatrix 384 vs NVL72: trading density for sheer count — 384 chips against 72 only works where power is cheap and advanced chips are scarce. In the end, total cost per token decides.
  • An NVL72 includes eight GPUs that never compute — A 64/72 slice means 523 racks for 32,768 active GPUs; goodput is the real unit of compute.
  • SXM or PCIe: beyond 700 W and 350 W, four constraints decide — NVIDIA's spec sheet: SXM up to 700 W with 900 GB/s NVLink and an HGX/DGX baseboard; NVL (PCIe) 350–400 W with a bridge between adjacent cards only. Parallelism, power, chassis and operations decide.
  • 36.8% utilisation: stop counting cards, start counting useful GPU-hours — CAICT puts 2025 supply near 3.8 billion GPU-hours against 1.4 billion used — 36.8%; Inspur monitored about 30% in Q1 2026. Separate utilisation, useful compute and goodput before adding capacity.
  • Vera Rubin bandwidth revised down 12.7%: which spec sheet do you trust — NVIDIA quietly cut HBM4 and NVLink figures in September; a Wccftech comparison surfaced it in late October. Development targets are not production specs.
  • Rubin's first production report card: 4.8x token throughput, mind the methodology — CoreWeave production, Cognition as first workload: 4.8x SWE-2 throughput and 10x per-megawatt DeepSeek-R1 — customer-reported figures, a strong signal but not a price list.

Fabrication, materials and lithography

Glass substrates are about flatness, not a new material

Intel shipped in January 2026; NVIDIA waits until 2028-2029. The money is in TGV drilling and metallisation.

  • Backside power buys back the voltage margin — PowerVia is in production on 18A; the price is 10-15% more wafer cost and a rewritten test flow.
  • Two announcements, one week: shipping now versus 2029 — After 1.35m wafers and 84% availability, the only dispute is whether EUR 350m per tool pays off.
  • An interface standard is dismantling the CoWoS premium — UCIe makes die-to-die public; the axis shifts from the leading node to system integration.
  • CoWoS hikes loom, substrate makers spend a record NT$161bn: costs cascade downstream — TSMC is reportedly weighing CoWoS price increases while the substrate trio commits a record NT$161.2bn and WFE heads past $200bn in 2027 — why cloud prices fall slower than chips improve.

Memory, packaging and interconnect

HBM4 will be won on the logic base die, not the DRAM stack

JESD270-4 sets a 2,048-bit interface at 2 TB/s. The three vendors are splitting over who fabricates the base die — and that decides who holds pricing power.

  • CPO's real competitor is not copper, but the pluggable optic that keeps improving — 144×800G and 102.4 Tbps are already shipping, while LPO claws back about 60% of the power advantage. The window for co-packaged optics is narrowing.
  • Open interconnect specs are two years ahead of the silicon — UALink 2.0 defines 1,024 accelerators per domain, but native switch silicon is a 2027 story. A published spec is not a purchasable part.
  • SOCAMM2 saves power, not wafers — The standard changes bytes per watt; it cannot change 270% server DRAM inflation or 20-40 week lead times.
  • Limited-volume 1.6T optics beats a CPO headline — Small-batch deliveries start in Q4; the strategic layer is the optical chip at over 30% of cost.

Power and the building

A 1 MW rack needs 200 kg of busbar: 800 V HVDC is a materials problem

NVIDIA's own figures: at 54 V, a 1 GW data centre would need 200 tonnes of copper for busbars alone.

  • 1,400 W per chip ends air cooling as an option — GB300 moves to full cold-plate liquid cooling with roughly 40% better energy efficiency. Data centres without liquid-cooling engineering are out of the bid.
  • 120 kW per rack: half the data centres are physically out — Asset value now follows kW per square metre, expandable power and liquid-cooling readiness — not floor area.
  • The grid queue is longer than the GPU lead time — The IEA counts more than 2,500 GW of projects waiting for interconnection; 45 TWh of PPAs were signed in the first half of 2026.
  • 132 GW cannot choose a rack for you — Between global gigawatts and rack kilowatts sit second-scale swings of tens of megawatts.
  • Four cost blocks in a liquid-cooling retrofit: do not compare cold-plate quotes — Cold plates run USD 5,000–15,000 per rack, a full rack deployment USD 30,000–50,000, and a 20-rack retrofit about USD 3.3m all-in. ROI turns near sustained 45–50 kW per rack above USD 0.11/kWh.
  • Slower than the grid queue: your neighbour's consent — Altman floats desert siting, OpenAI's CFO lists power, water, noise and community paybacks, CBRE wants lease guarantors for startups. Social licence now gates timelines like power does.
  • 10% to 150%: the hard part of an AI rack is not the power draw, it is the timescale — GPU racks jump from 10% to 150% of rated power in milliseconds while grid resources take 1–90 minutes. Three storage layers cover different gaps; what they buy is power on time.

Inference cost and software stacks

The real barrier for China's accelerators is porting hours, not peak FLOPS

Compatibility claims top 90%, yet TensorRT-LLM is unsupported on all three major domestic stacks. Reworking kernels and schedulers is what actually costs.

  • 56%: the share inflection that says more than an export ban — Domestic share rose from 46% to 56%, with 4 million accelerators shipped in 2025, 812,000 of them Ascend. The question is no longer whether they work, but what room NVIDIA keeps.
  • Behind 1.55 million tokens/second: inference economics is repricing GPUs — Selling FLOPS is giving way to selling tokens; total cost per million tokens has become the common currency of compute procurement.
  • Why 2,000 tokens/second is not a comparable number — The same model varies 18-fold across independent leaderboards, vendor benchmarks claim over 3,000 t/s, and dedicated inference chips rarely appear in MLPerf at all.
  • Long context costs more in memory than in compute — Mooncake's production trace serves 75% more requests; a 40 GB KV cache moves at 190 GB/s over 400G RoCE.
  • HBM up 3%, throughput claimed at 30x — Discount the vendor benchmark, but useful work per byte has replaced capacity as the metric.
  • Moore Threads posts its first profitable quarter: domestic GPUs now report, not just present — Q3 revenue of RMB 1.55bn, up 1,773.92% YoY — the first quarterly profit since its December 2025 IPO. The yardstick shifts from demos to statements.

Open silicon and dev boards

Open silicon sells auditability, not price/performance

Blackhole pairs GDDR6 with a full Apache 2.0 stack, and the p100a sells for $999. Being able to inspect the chip is harder to substitute than a spec.

  • You cannot divide 6 TOPS by 26 TOPS — Sparse, dense and architecture-specific figures get mixed freely; in independent benchmarks the cheaper board often leads on frames per dollar.
  • No H100 budget? Start with a dev board — From a low-cost MCU to the 67 TOPS Jetson Orin Nano Super, plus a simple rule: if 24 months of cloud bills exceed the board, buy the board.
  • A 360 MHz MCU without an NPU runs 65.7m parameters — Toolchains and vector instructions redraw the line; a 2.9s load and 32 MB PSRAM mark its edge.
  • Edge AI is a different business, not a small data centre — A USD 34.4bn market, fragmented NPU toolchains, and ever-cheaper cloud tokens competing for the same work.
  • The gate between generation and flashing is the hard part — Testable state rules, manifest checks and a pinned toolchain are worth more than the schematic.

Inspection, faults and grading

Used H100 inspection: turn “is it working” into a reproducible checklist

The price gap is not about benchmark scores, it is about whether a buyer can reproduce the card's history: ECC and page-retirement records, row-remapping headroom, DCGM L3/L4, link state and 48 hours at full load, all tied to the serial number.

  • What belongs in a DCGM health report: a template the buyer can re-run — DCGM L1 takes seconds, L2 about two minutes, L3 about 15 and L4 about 90. The value is not the verdict but device identity, versions, per-test pass/fail, ECC counters and the exact commands to reproduce it.
  • Xid 79 is not a verdict: triage the code before you replace the card — NVIDIA defines Xid 79 as the driver losing PCIe access to the GPU — link, hardware or driver. Work through the 13/31/48/63-64/74/79/94-95/140 family, preserve evidence first, then decide whether to repair.
  • A training job lost a GPU? It was probably not the GPU — Meta's 16,384 H100s logged 419 failures in 54 days, and GPU plus HBM3 caused barely half of the interruptions. Reproduce on one node, check versions, InfiniBand and topology before reaching for DCGM.
  • From fault code to spare part number: the three matching layers — The same functional part carries different numbers across server vendors and chassis revisions; Dell requires matching H100 heatsinks by service tag. A structured symptom-to-part-to-channel table is the real moat.
  • Why a grading report adds 15–20% to a used accelerator — ITAD vendors report a 15–20% premium for graded stock over as-is units; Introl's 1,534-card study puts the H100 80GB near 70% of launch price. The premium prices uncertainty, not extra speed.
  • What an H100 hour actually costs: six markets, six answers — $0.66 to $11.06 per GPU-hour, with residual value estimated at just 10–20% of the original price.
  • A one-person company in the compute aftermarket: coordination costs shrink, liability costs do not — Aggregation and content can be automated; grading, RMA decisions, spare-part matching and cross-border paperwork need a signature. Default Agent blocking adds supply-cut risk to automated acquisition.

Components and cost arithmetic

Four parameters everyone misses when substituting a component

Separate pin-to-pin, functional and parametric compatibility first. The failures come from temperature derating and drift, power-up sequencing, batch consistency, and qualification plus lifecycle status.

  • In a shortage, the expensive part is not the price rise — it is “close enough” — Passing at room temperature but failing at extremes, nominal specs with the derating margin gone, and a sample batch that production cannot match: set verification depth by part class before discussing lead time.
  • From one board to 100 units: which BOM lines actually change — At prototype stage a BOM is a shopping list; at 100 units it becomes a cost structure. Split lines into amortising, linear and one-off costs to see which unit prices fall and why spare allowance rises.
  • RTX 5090 above $4,300: gamers are paying the HBM capacity tax — Memory makers are shifting wafers to HBM; GDDR7 and mainstream DRAM tighten in turn, and server vendors now list memory shortages in their risk disclosures.
  • The AI chip shortage is often a packaging shortage — Monthly capacity has doubled to 120k–140k wafers and a gap of roughly 20% remains. Compute expansion starts with a packaging allocation.
  • Germanium doubles in a year: minor metals on the control list choke optics and IR — The 28 October ban on gallium, germanium and antimony exports to the US landed into a 40–50 tonne supply gap and 30-odd tonnes of inventory. Treat Ge/Ga/Sb BOM lines as shortage-grade risk.

Capital, markets and supply chains

80% margins, 45% drawdown: memory earnings and share prices part ways

Samsung topped 100 trillion KRW in quarterly operating profit while memory stocks fell ~45% from highs. The pricing curve is flattening: DRAM ASPs +23% in Q3, guided 10–15% in Q4.

  • A $161bn lease: custom silicon act two opens with debt — Anthropic's S-1 discloses five-year $161bn equipment leases with Broadcom — half of a 32 GW pipeline — financed via the largest AI debt deal on record.
  • $20bn in one day at 8.65% coupons: the credit market prices AI buildout — Oracle priced $10bn equity at an 11.2% discount plus $10bn bonds up to 8.65% — some 300bp above comparable investment grade. Spreads are the soberer AI-cycle gauge.
  • The $3 trillion revenue gap: two multiplied twos in the AI payback math — Chip spend ×2 for the data centre, ×2 for cloud margin: $1.5tn of 2026 buildout needs ~$3tn of end-user revenue against an estimated $133–200bn of actual AI revenue.
  • The same H100: six years on the books, twenty-odd percent on the market after three — Burry estimates about USD 176bn of understated depreciation, while Amazon and Meta moved billions in opposite directions. CoreWeave renewal quotes and used A100 prices tell the other half.

Frontier compute and networks

NVIDIA pays $1bn for 2.9% of Nokia: base stations are becoming data centres

On 21 October a call over a commercial 5G network ran radio processing on GPU servers. Lab gains of 10–15% and a 2028 doubling target — keep the measurement scopes apart.

  • 8,192 GH200s simulate 104 qubits: the fault-tolerance rehearsal passed, the hardware has not — JUPITER set the classical record simulating magic-state cultivation, cutting physical qubit estimates by ~90%. NVQLink deployments suggest hybrid systems capture the first value.

Agent access, identity and settlement

After September 15: what is blocked by default is not “AI crawlers”, but a purpose

Since 15 September 2026 Training and Agent traffic is blocked by default on ad pages while Search stays allowed. The axis moved from crawler names to purposes, and mixed-use Googlebot inherits the strictest rule.

  • Content Signals is a declaration, not a gate: do not tape the lock drawing to the door — robots.txt governs fetching; Content Signals adds what may happen afterwards, but it remains a preference statement. Only edge enforcement produces a status code.
  • llms.txt: publishing grew 8.8x, and 97% of the files were never requested once — Sites publishing the file rose from 4,088 to 36,120 in a year, yet in Ahrefs' 137,000-domain sample 97% saw zero requests. Publishing and fetching move in opposite directions.
  • From 402 to Pay Per Use: the unit being priced is no longer the crawl, but confirmed use — HTTP 402 and Web Bot Auth made fetches priceable in 2025; from July 2026 billing follows appearance in an answer. Pricing power did not move back to publishers.
  • The MCP trust gap: a tool description looks almost like a system prompt to the model — MCP was built for interoperability, not as a security boundary. Of six attack surfaces, three deliver instructions into context and three decide what they can achieve. Admission, version monitoring and least privilege are the gates.
  • A signature proves which key signed it: Web Bot Auth does not grant permission — The IETF adopted the -00 draft in September 2026. A signature does not say who operates the agent or whether the request was authorised, and the draft does not require covering the body. Identity, authorisation and intent are three layers.
  • x402, TAP, AP2 and MPP are not a four-way choice: they govern four layers — Settlement rails, identity credentials, intent authorisation and merchant integration are distinct layers, and the protocols mostly compose. For a mainland China entity, acquiring qualification and invoicing come first.
  • A 402 price header is not an invoice: turn one request into one auditable entry — A 402 is only a quote. Verification result, quote version, idempotency key, payment proof, delivery result and compensation path must sit in one record. Onchain finality has no chargeback.
  • From ranking to recommended: the unit of AEO is not a citation count — Cloudflare put Agent Readiness and AEO on the agenda in August 2026, but citation share is observational. A screenshot proves one moment; only first-party trends prove sustained visibility.

Component data engineering

Behind 180 million datasheets: what an open component database cannot buy is the licence

A commercial service lists 180m+ datasheets and 76m+ substitution relations with manufacturer licensing and live updates. An open project should first separate three classes of data by licence and shelf life.

  • Manage “what I have” before “what exists”: the starting order Part-DB demonstrates — Part-DB under AGPL-3.0 joins inventory, barcodes, BOMs and KiCad, with suppliers as optional feeds. A bounded set plus a real validation loop beats chasing row counts.
  • An MPN is not a primary key: component identity needs at least four layers — One part-number string can cover different packages, grades and reel formats — exactly where substitution decisions turn. Using it as a unique key scales one error across the whole BOM.
  • From batch to individual die: advanced packaging turned traceability granularity into a product — SEMI T23, T25 and T26 point toward individual device identity and controlled data exchange. ECIDs can be copied; PUF and root-of-trust designs are unclonable but cost area, power and testability.
  • NRND is not EOL: component lifecycle does not belong in one enum field — NXP distinguishes EOL from NLM and TI lists NRND, LTB and obsolete separately. Store notices, revisions and evidence as events; status is only a derivation as of a given date.
  • A PCN is not a discontinuance notice: evidence starts with document type — Merge change and discontinuance notices into one field and downstream users cannot tell an FFF review from a last-time-buy calculation. Separate document type, scope, four dates and recommended replacements.
  • A PDF saying REACH compliant is not data: compliance fields need the list version — The SVHC list updates twice a year and the declaration standards change revision. A compliance boolean makes the whole database expire at once when the list changes.
  • A last-time buy is not buying extra: compare it against three other paths — Four routes follow a discontinuance notice: LTB, remaining authorised stock, an approved alternate or redesign. Approving the order and releasing the lot are two separate decisions.

Commissioning and measurement

Cold-plate acceptance is not powering it on: fix the TCS/FWS boundary first

Secondary and facility loops need separate records. Flush endpoints, water chemistry, leak response, heat balance and IT-meter reconciliation all have to be in place before handover.

  • The PUE dispute is not about the formula, it is about where the denominator is drawn — Liquid cooling moves CDU pumps and server fans across the IT/facility line. Calling 1.5 to 1.3 a 20 percent saving mistakes a ratio change for a savings rate.
  • Leak acceptance tests the action chain, not the sensor — An alarm is not a GPU power reduction. Pressure regime, detection-to-actuation time and quick-disconnect cross-verification decide whether a leak becomes an outage.
  • An acceptance pack is not a report: turn it runs into it can be rechecked — Hand over a reproducible operating baseline: raw trends, calibration records, sample reports, test methods, deviations and signed retests. Miss one and later disputes cannot get back to the facts.
