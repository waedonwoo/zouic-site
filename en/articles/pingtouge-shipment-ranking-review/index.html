560,000 Units and IDC Second Place: Two Different Framings

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

When the PingTouGe name comes up, two numbers keep being placed side by side: cumulative shipments of 560,000 units and a second place on the IDC list. The first comes from the company's own disclosure, the second from a third-party annual ranking, and in between sit a spin-off listing application, a fundraising amount and a valuation. These are different kinds of things, yet they are read as one line of results.

The spin-off filing and two shipment figures

Let us align the events first. Alibaba announced on 10 November 2026 that it had filed an application with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange to spin off PingTouGe for an independent listing; PingTouGe filed confidentially between September and November, and after the spin-off it remains an Alibaba subsidiary. Cited repeatedly over the same period is the figure of 560,000 Zhenwu series units shipped cumulatively as of April 2026, serving more than 20 industries and over 400 customers, including more than 130,000 cards for intelligent driving and 100,000 cards for finance. Another set is IDC's 2025 ranking of cloud AI accelerators in China, which put Huawei first with 812,000 units shipped and PingTouGe second with 265,000. One announcement, one company disclosure and one third-party ranking sit in three different layers of material.

Three framings get added together

Why is it so easy to mix them up? Because 560,000 and 265,000 are both chip counts and look comparable. But 560,000 is a self-reported cumulative shipment with a cutoff of April 2026 covering the Zhenwu series, while 265,000 is a third-party estimate of shipments in the single calendar year 2025 for China's cloud AI accelerator market, with a different scope and a different period. One is an odometer reading, the other a sales chart for one year; the numerators and denominators are not the same. Subtracting or adding the two yields only a framing mismatch, not a meaningful difference. Add to that annualized revenue in the tens of billions, a proposed raise of USD 2 to 3 billion and a valuation of USD 25 to 62 billion; these are forecasts and quotes and belong to a different order of magnitude from the first two sets.

Who discloses, who ranks, who forecasts

There are several kinds of actors in this chain of numbers. Alibaba is the disclosing party for the spin-off, with the announcement filed to the Hong Kong Stock Exchange, which makes it tier 2 official company information. Shipments of the Zhenwu series and the industry and customer counts are disclosed by the company, partly broadcast by CCTV in September 2025 and, after relay by media, enter reporting; these are also checked as tier 2. IDC is a research institute whose ranking enters public reporting through media relay and is treated as tier 4, a directional reference only. The statement about annualized revenue in the tens of billions comes from company management, while the IFR account of a 2027 Hong Kong IPO raise and the JP Morgan valuation come from third-party compilations and broker forecasts, likewise treated as tier 4. Establishing who is speaking settles half the weight of a number.

The time points all differ

Time points must be aligned one by one. The spin-off filing was on 10 November 2026 and the confidential submission falls between September and November; the 560,000 cumulative shipment figure has a cutoff of April 2026; the IDC ranking covers the full year 2025; the Zhenwu PPU with 96GB of HBM2e and 700GB/s die-to-die interconnect appeared on CCTV in September 2025; the Zhenwu M890 launched in May with 144GB, 800GB/s and three times the 810E; the Zhenwu V900 was unveiled at the September Yunqi event with mass production expected in the first quarter of 2027; and the T-Head SAIL software stack was open-sourced in July. The as-of date of 22 November 2026 is the checking point, not the publication date of these figures. Putting numbers from different dates into one table amounts to assuming they all hold at once.

Which layer of material the numbers sit in

Which layer of material a number sits in determines how it can be cited. The spin-off filing sits in the Hong Kong Stock Exchange announcement, a checkable official document. Shipment and customer counts sit in company disclosures and CCTV material and lose one notch of certainty after media relay. Product framings sit in exhibition and launch materials: the PPU is 96GB of HBM2e with 700GB/s die-to-die interconnect, the M890 is 144GB with 800GB/s and three times the 810E, the V900 is expected to enter mass production in the first quarter of 2027, and the SAIL software stack was open-sourced in July. The IDC ranking sits in third-party market estimates relayed by media and is treated as tier 4. The fundraising and valuation sit in third-party compilations and broker forecasts. What can enter definite conclusions is the checkable announcement and disclosure; estimates and forecasts serve only as direction.

Three steps to separate two framings

Three steps take this set of numbers apart. Step one, ask about nature: is the figure a self-reported cumulative, a third-party annual ranking, or a fundraising and valuation forecast? The three are written in different ledgers. Step two, align time and scope: cumulative to which day, annual for which year, covering which products and markets, and if they are not aligned there is no comparison. Step three, set the purpose: whether the question is about shipment scale, market position or capital expectations, and each takes its own evidence. Treat these three sets of numbers as three different tables: cumulative shipments are like an odometer, the IDC ranking like one year's sales chart, and the fundraising valuation like a listing price; nobody adds an odometer to a sales chart. Only after the three steps does the number enter a judgment.

A spin-off filing is not a completed listing

Several boundaries need holding. A spin-off filing only shows that a process has started; it does not mean the listing is complete, nor that pricing and fundraising are settled. The 560,000 is a cumulative shipment figure, not a single-year 2025 shipment and still less the 265,000 of IDC framing; the two cannot be added and cannot corroborate each other. The IDC ranking is a third-party estimate relayed by media, treated as tier 4, a directional reference only and not entering definite conclusions. Annualized revenue in the tens of billions, the proposed USD 2 to 3 billion Hong Kong IPO raise in 2027 and the USD 25 to 62 billion valuation are all forecasts or quotes, not events that have happened. The V900 mass production in the first quarter of 2027 is an expectation and cannot be written as already in production either.

Keep three sets of accounts separate

What this material confirms is this: Alibaba announced on 10 November 2026 the filing of an application to spin off PingTouGe for an independent listing, and after the spin-off it remains an Alibaba subsidiary; as of April 2026 the Zhenwu series had shipped 560,000 units cumulatively, serving more than 20 industries and over 400 customers, with more than 130,000 cards for intelligent driving and 100,000 for finance; in IDC's 2025 ranking PingTouGe placed second with 265,000 units shipped and Huawei first with 812,000. The 560,000 is a self-reported cumulative, the 265,000 a third-party annual ranking, and the fundraising and valuation are forecasts, three framings that cannot be added or used to corroborate one another. On the product side, what can be checked are the public framings of the PPU, M890, V900 and SAIL, while V900 mass production remains an expectation. A spin-off filing is not a completed listing, and that is the single most important thing to remember when reading these results.

Frequently asked questions

Can the 560,000 and IDC's 265,000 be added or compared?

No. The 560,000 is the company's disclosed cumulative shipment of the Zhenwu series as of April 2026, serving more than 20 industries and over 400 customers, with more than 130,000 cards for intelligent driving and 100,000 for finance; it is a self-reported cumulative framing. IDC's 265,000 is an annual shipment ranking for China's cloud AI accelerator market in 2025, with Huawei first at 812,000 and PingTouGe second; it is a third-party estimate relayed by media and treated as tier 4. The two differ in period, scope and source, so they cannot be added and cannot corroborate each other, and serve only as a directional reference.

Does a spin-off filing mean PingTouGe is already listed?

No. What Alibaba announced on 10 November 2026 was the filing of an application with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange to spin off PingTouGe for an independent listing; PingTouGe filed confidentially between September and November, and after the spin-off it remains an Alibaba subsidiary. A filing only starts the process; whether the listing is completed, when it is priced and how much is raised all depend on later announcements. IFR says a 2027 Hong Kong IPO is planned to raise USD 2 to 3 billion and JP Morgan values it at USD 25 to 62 billion; these are third-party compilations and broker forecasts and do not enter definite conclusions.
