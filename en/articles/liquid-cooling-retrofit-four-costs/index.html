Four cost blocks in a liquid-cooling retrofit: do not compare cold-plate quotes

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

The most misleading thing about a liquid-cooling quote is the unit "per rack". A cold plate is one line on the bill. CDUs, secondary-loop piping, manifolds, leak detection, power upgrades, fire-suppression changes and commissioning frequently cost as much as the cooling hardware itself. The building sets the price, not the equipment.

What the four blocks are

  • Cooling equipment: direct-to-chip cold-plate hardware at about USD 5,000–15,000 per rack; a complete rack-level deployment with CDU, manifold and liquid-ready chassis at USD 30,000–50,000.
  • Facility work: primary loop, electrical distribution upgrades, leak detection, isolation valves, dry-break quick disconnects and clean-agent fire suppression — routinely underestimated.
  • Downtime window: field playbooks for phased retrofits allow 4–8 hours of downtime per pilot rack, with conversion waves of 5–10 racks inside maintenance windows and 12–18 months end to end.
  • Long-term operations: coolant chemistry and filtration, CDU filter changes, quarterly water-quality testing and quick-disconnect mating cycles (the industry commonly specifies 10,000).

A directional estimate for 20 racks puts the all-in figure near USD 3.3 million. One published Chinese 100-rack retrofit lists about RMB 5.2m of equipment, RMB 6.25m total and a 6.7-year payback — but that site started at only 2.5 kW per rack, a completely different density baseline from an AI hall. The two figures cannot be compared horizontally; they only show how wide the definitional gap is.

Cross the threshold before choosing a design

A useful boundary from 2026 TCO models: liquid cooling turns ROI-positive in well-utilised facilities with sustained densities of 45–50 kW per rack and electricity above roughly USD 0.11/kWh. Below about 30 kW, modern variable-speed air cooling still wins. Air-cooled halls commonly run at PUE 1.5–1.67 versus 1.05–1.20 for direct-to-chip, but the electricity saved does not automatically cover the enabling work.

Three engineering constraints also apply: older facilities were deliberately designed to keep water away from IT racks, and colocation SLAs may even prohibit water near equipment, so contracts need revising first; if the facility water loop cannot absorb the CDU heat load, adding a dry cooler later raises the budget by 30–50%; and glycol-water mixtures degrade into corrosive acids that eat cold-plate microchannels, so filtration and quarterly chemistry checks are not optional.

A steadier path

Carve out 2–3 non-production high-density racks for a 30-day pilot and log coolant inlet/outlet temperatures, pressure drop and component thermals. Replicate the same building block pod by pod, and only extend the primary loop once the load justifies it. That spreads capital and avoids over-plumbing for the 120 kW racks in the press releases.

If the site is still struggling with utilisation, cooling may not be the priority at all: the utilisation metric piece describes a cheaper form of expansion. All figures here are public engineering estimates and listing prices; scopes differ sharply between sources and every project has to be recalculated against its own building.

In short

Liquid cooling is not a hardware choice, it is a question of density threshold plus building condition plus downtime cost. Comparing cold-plate unit prices alone is basically not doing the maths.

Frequently asked questions

Roughly what does an air-to-liquid retrofit cost per rack?

Two layers: cold-plate and related cooling hardware at about USD 5,000–15,000 per rack, and a full rack-level deployment including CDU, manifolds and liquid-ready chassis at USD 30,000–50,000. Power, piping, fire suppression and commissioning are additional and often comparable to the hardware.

At what density does a retrofit pay off?

2026 TCO models commonly put the ROI threshold at sustained 45–50 kW per rack with electricity above about USD 0.11/kWh and high utilisation. Below roughly 30 kW per rack, variable-speed air cooling is usually more economical.

Is downtime unavoidable?

Almost always. Phased plans typically convert 5–10 racks per maintenance window, with 4–8 hours of downtime per pilot rack; the full programme is more realistically scheduled over 12–18 months.
