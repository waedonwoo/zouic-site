You cannot divide 6 TOPS by 26 TOPS: three measurement traps in edge AI selection

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

Budgets for running models locally can be tiered, but there is a more basic problem: the TOPS figures in those spec sheets are often not the same unit at all. Selecting on them goes wrong at step one. Back to the budget-path article

Start with an honest independent benchmark

The RoboKrunch edge AI benchmark repository on GitHub (September 2026) states it plainly: the FPS figures below come from vendor datasheets, not laboratory reproductions, and are published precisely to show how messy the units are.

Orange Pi 5 Max 8GBRK3588, 6 INT8 TOPSYOLOv5s, 66.1 FPSUSD 95 / 0.70

Three numbers, three measurement bases: Rockchip's 6 is INT8, Hailo's 26 is dense INT8, NVIDIA's 40 is sparse INT8, on different architectures — and even the YOLO variants tested differ (v5s versus v5m). The repository author states the conclusion directly: dividing TOPS across vendors is directionally useful but precisely wrong. More counter-intuitively, on FPS per dollar the USD 75 RK3588 board actually leads a set of far more expensive modules.

The second trap: NPUs cannot run generative models

The same benchmark repository contains a measurement worth copying into any selection note: the community ran Qwen2.5-0.5B on the RK3588 CPU with llama.cpp at about 12 tokens/s, explicitly labelled a demo, not a product strategy — the NPU simply cannot run generative workloads. That is a different question from whether 6 TOPS can run an LLM: the operator set, memory bandwidth and toolchain of most edge NPUs cover CNN / vision inference only, and the TOPS figure never promised Transformer decode.

Compare the EdgeAIStack platform roundup from August 2026 (prices are guidance and concurrent stream counts are planning estimates, not measurements): Jetson Orin Nano Super at 67 TOPS / 10-25 W / around USD 399, Orin NX 16GB at 157 TOPS / around USD 999, AGX Thor T5000 at 2070 FP4 TFLOPS / 40-130 W / around USD 4,999; Hailo-8L at 13 TOPS / 1-1.5 W / around USD 70, RK3588 around USD 149, Google Coral at 4 TOPS / around USD 60. Prices span almost two orders of magnitude, but whether the board can run the model you need does not rise monotonically with price.

The third trap: which of the four layers of open hardware are actually open

In the RISC-V camp the Milk-V Mars (about USD 50), VisionFive 2 (about USD 65) and PINE64 Star64 all use the same JH7110; a third-party ranking measured Node.js cold starts at around 18 seconds, with latency on RISC-V roughly twice that of a Pi 5. The Raspberry Pi 5 (about USD 80) has the strongest community and documentation but is not, strictly, open hardware. RK3588 boards offer the widest choice and the best compute per dollar, at the cost of uneven BSP and community support; BeagleY-AI takes the industrial real-time I/O route.

So open has to be asked as four separate questions: are schematics public, is the BSP/device tree upstreamed, can firmware and bootloader be replaced, and are the SDK and model conversion tools locked to a single vendor. Many boards open only the first layer. For a project heading into volume production, BSP quality and community size often determine the delivery schedule more than an extra 10 TOPS — choosing a board is really choosing a software supply chain you will live with for three to five years.

Bottom line

The right order for edge AI selection is: lock down the model and operators first, confirm the target SDK's support matrix, then check power and thermals, and only then look at TOPS and price. Claims that 6 TOPS is not enough, or that 40 TOPS must be faster, are illusions created by forcing different units into one table. To actually finish the project, ask one question before buying: can this board's NPU compile the model I need?

Frequently asked questions

Can TOPS from different vendors be compared directly?

No. Sparse INT8, dense INT8, different architectures and different precisions are not the same dimension. An independent benchmark repository explicitly labels cross-vendor conversion as directionally useful but precisely wrong, and recommends comparing FPS on the same model and input size, plus FPS per dollar.

Can the RK3588's 6 TOPS NPU run a large language model?

Usually not. Edge NPU operator sets and toolchains target vision/CNN inference; community tests run Qwen2.5-0.5B at about 12 tokens/s on the RK3588 CPU with llama.cpp, which is a demo rather than a product design.

What should I check with an open-source dev board?

Split open into four layers: schematics, BSP/device tree, firmware, and SDK. Many boards publish only schematics while drivers and model conversion tools stay closed, which directly affects long-term maintenance and production feasibility.
