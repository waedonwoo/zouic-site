A $161bn lease: custom silicon act two opens with debt

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

By 2026 the custom-chip story is no longer about tape-out capability. Broadcom booked $16.7bn of AI semiconductor revenue in its fiscal third quarter, up 221% year on year, and Anthropic's listing documents then revealed the structure behind those numbers: five-year equipment leases worth $161bn, plus a $63bn chip purchase financed by four institutions. Act two of the compute race opens with debt.

The S-1 reveals the order book behind custom silicon

According to Anthropic's filing and multiple reports in early November: Anthropic has five-year equipment leases with Broadcom totalling $161bn, roughly half of the about 32GW of compute Broadcom expects to sell through early 2028 — on that pace Anthropic could become its largest XPU customer next year. The financing structure is just as large: a $63bn chip transaction between the two was arranged by Bank of America, Citigroup and Morgan Stanley on the senior side with Blackstone providing the second-lien portion, which the Wall Street Journal called the largest AI debt deal on record; the banks plan to sell that debt into the bond market next year.

Broadcom's own results anchor the revenue side of those promises: fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $29.59bn, up 86% year on year, including $16.7bn of AI semiconductor revenue, up 221%; CEO Hock Tan's framing is roughly $58bn of AI revenue for FY2026, then two consecutive doublings — about $115bn in FY2027 and $230bn in FY2028. The Google TPU partnership is now explicitly extended to 2031, Ironwood (seventh-generation TPU) is shipping in volume for both Google and Anthropic, and the eighth-generation, inference-optimised v8i has begun delivery.

The lease structure reroutes the risk

Under this arrangement the equipment belongs to the financing vehicle, the banks' first lien is on the hardware itself, and Broadcom provides residual-value support. Anthropic locks in capacity without paying cash up front; the cost is written in its own filing: as of December 2025 its future compute commitments totalled about $417bn, first-half 2026 infrastructure spending ran $8.9bn, up 283% year on year, against $16.3bn of revenue and an $80.7bn net loss (mostly non-cash fair-value movements on financial instruments). The chip designer is simultaneously lessor and potential financier of the same kit — Anthropic's own filing warns investors to scrutinise the multiple roles.

The industry-level signal is equally clear: Omdia expects custom chips to overtake GPUs by unit volume as early as 2028. But note two easily missed details. First, Broadcom guided gross margin down from 78% to 73% for fiscal Q4, officially because memory-intensive XPUs are taking a larger share — custom silicon is a scale business, not a high-margin one. Second, the 3.5GW Anthropic has locked on Google TPUs only starts delivering in 2027; the commitment precedes the silicon by a full supply-chain ramp.

Summary

The $161bn lease changes the question about custom chips. It is no longer "how close does XPU peak performance get to GPU" but whether a set of long-dated leases, a large structured debt deal and a technical partnership running to 2031 can stand together through cycle volatility. The build-versus-buy question is quietly becoming a credit-analysis question.

Frequently asked questions

What is Anthropic's $161bn lease?

Per its S-1 filing and multiple press reports, it is a five-year equipment lease with Broadcom covering about half of a roughly 32GW compute pipeline. The equipment is held by a financing vehicle, with a bank syndicate providing senior debt and Blackstone the second-lien portion — together with a separate $63bn chip sale it is described as the largest AI debt deal on record.

How fast is Broadcom's AI revenue growing?

Fiscal Q3 2026 AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7bn, up 221% year on year, with Q4 guided to $21.7bn. Management's mid-term framing: about $58bn for FY2026, roughly $115bn in FY2027 and $230bn in FY2028. These are company guidance figures, not booked orders.

Will custom chips replace GPUs?

Omdia expects custom chips to overtake GPUs by unit volume as early as 2028, but their positioning differs: XPUs are deeply bound to one customer's workloads and financing structure, while GPUs retain generality and resale value. For most non-hyperscale buyers, the options and negotiating room remain entirely different.
