Moore Threads posts its first profitable quarter: domestic GPUs now file reports, not just presentations

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

A GPU company turning a single-quarter profit in its first listing year is rare anywhere in the world. Moore Threads' Q3 2026 report on 29 October showed net profit attributable to shareholders turned positive year on year for both the third quarter and the first nine months — its first profitable quarter since listing on 5 December 2025, and the second "little dragon" of domestic GPU to manage it. The industry implication matters more: the yardstick is switching from "which models can it run" to "which statements can it file".

Three signals in the statement

First, the revenue structure is cashing in: nine-month revenue of RMB 3.289bn, up 319.15% year on year, of which Q3 alone was RMB 1.552bn, up 1,773.92%. In the 4 November investor survey the company attributed growth to AI demand for high-performance compute and full-function GPUs, with MTT S5000-class intelligence-compute products landing at internet and cloud providers, token service providers, carriers and intelligent-computing centres. The three-line framing around model-training factories, token-production factories and agent-production factories is the narrative it repeats.

Second, operating leverage is appearing: nine-month R&D expense of RMB 1.34bn, up 55.7% — revenue growth of 319% far outpaces R&D growth, and selling and administrative expense ratios have both dropped to single digits. Receivables turnover of 59 days is healthy by A-share technology standards, and it quietly answers a common suspicion: how much of the domestic compute revenue is real cash collection. Third, the market's positive feedback: on 4 November the stock rose 13.60% to RMB 335.71, a market cap of RMB 157.8bn. In the same period Muxi's IPO tutoring moved to "tutoring completed" — two of the four little dragons have now finished or nearly finished the listing process.

The real questions after profitability

A single profitable quarter is not a stable profit engine. The GPU industry is doubly capital- and technology-intensive, scale effects set the break-even point, and one large customer's purchasing rhythm can reverse a quarter — every non-NVIDIA peer globally has learned this. Moore Threads' stated differentiation is "full-function": AI compute, graphics rendering, video codec and physics simulation in one die, extended into embodied AI (the open-sourced MuJoCo Warp MUSA simulation backend, a Wuxi industrial embodied-AI innovation centre with 16 ecosystem partners). The cost of that route is a wide R&D front; the benefit is scenario redundancy when demand wobbles.

For coming quarters, watch two ratios rather than "profitable or not": the gap between R&D expense growth and revenue growth (whether the break-even point keeps moving right), and the change in top-five customer concentration (whether this round of domestic-substitution demand has breadth). The first shows management's discipline between profit and investment; the second shows the width of demand.

Summary

Moore Threads' first profit marks the domestic GPU sector moving from a financing race to a statement race. The differentiation to come will resemble solar a decade ago or EV batteries five years ago: once the listing window opens, capital asks harsher questions — not "can you build it" but "who keeps buying, do they pay, and is there profit after collection". A company that answers for three consecutive quarters is the one that has truly landed.

Frequently asked questions

What are Moore Threads' profit figures?

Per the 29 October 2026 Q3 report and China Fund News: nine-month revenue of RMB 3.289bn, up 319.15% year on year; Q3 revenue of RMB 1.552bn, up 1,773.92%; net profit attributable to shareholders turned positive year on year for both Q3 and the nine months — the first since its December 2025 listing.

Where do the "four little dragons" of domestic GPU stand?

Moore Threads listed on 5 December 2025 (issue price RMB 114.28) and posted its first quarterly profit in Q3 2026 — the second of the four to do so; Muxi's IPO tutoring status has moved to "tutoring completed"; Enflame and Biren remain at the filing stage.

Does one profitable quarter prove the domestic GPU business model works?

Not yet. The GPU industry is capital- and technology-intensive with strong scale effects, and a single large customer's purchasing rhythm can flip a quarter. Track the gap between R&D expense growth and revenue growth, top-five customer concentration, and receivables turnover (59 days for Moore Threads' first nine months) — only several consecutive quarters of verification make it robust.
