HPC Procurement: Separate Three Dates First

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

At SC26 on 16 November 2026 NVIDIA gave three things at once: HPC SDK 26.11, which is available now; the Vera CPU rack, expected to ship in early 2027; and the Rubin HPC GPU, expected in 2028. The three sit at different stages and cannot be merged into one claim of general availability. Rack power and key GPU specifications such as memory capacity, FP64 throughput and power are unpublished, so scientific computing procurement needs to ask about these gaps before writing specifications. The as-of date is 21 November 2026.

What: Three things, three states

According to the NVIDIA press release and official blog, Vera CPU is a rack-scale platform with 192 Vera CPUs, Quantum-X800 InfiniBand interconnect and a fully liquid-cooled MGX design; aggregate peak memory bandwidth above 200TB/s, about 300TB of memory and 1PB of storage; each 1U tray carries eight CPUs plus NVMe and management; and it reuses the NVL72 rack chassis, power distribution and cooling components. Each Vera CPU has 88 custom Olympus cores, 176 threads, SOCAMM2 LPDDR5X and up to 1.2TB/s bandwidth per CPU. HPC SDK 26.11 is available now, and the next-generation Fortran compiler is in public beta.

Why: Available, expected and roadmap are three things

HPC SDK 26.11 is available software; the Vera CPU rack is expected from Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro starting in early 2027, which is expected supply; the Rubin HPC GPU is expected in 2028, which is a roadmap. The press release contains an explicit forward-looking statement that products and timing may change and are not a commitment. Writing all three as one claim of general availability flattens software availability, expected systems and a chip roadmap into a single state, and procurement specifications lose their time scale.

Who: Who supplies and who selects

The rack is expected from Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro. The German climate computing centre DKRZ has selected Vera CPU for HLRE-5, built by Bull and expected to be available in early 2028, alongside GB200 and Quantum-2; this is a user-side selection and expected date, not something that can be purchased now. Performance figures come from NVIDIA self-testing: the OpenFOAM motorBike Large airflow simulation reaches 1.9 times the performance of an x86 system and 2.4 times the performance per watt, while the WRF model is projected at 1.6 times the performance and 2 times the energy efficiency, a vendor projection. The press release does not name the specific x86 comparison model.

When: Three dates laid out

16 November 2026 is the SC26 launch date, when HPC SDK 26.11 became available; early 2027 is the expected time for the Vera CPU rack from four suppliers; early 2028 is the expected availability of DKRZ HLRE-5; and 2028 is the expected launch of the Rubin HPC GPU. The as-of date of 21 November 2026 is when the site checked the reporting, not the launch or supply date. Laid out in time, the boundaries between available, expected and roadmap are clear; compressed into one claim of general availability, they disappear.

Where: Which material carries the figures

Platform composition, interconnect and memory bandwidth sit in the official press release; the performance multiples sit in NVIDIA self-tests of OpenFOAM and WRF; the Rubin HPC GPU figures of more than 50,000 CUDA cores per GPU, acceleration for sparse data and seventh-generation NVLink sit in the official blog from the same event, which gives no memory capacity, FP64 throughput, power or system configuration; and the DKRZ selection and HLRE-5 timing sit in user-side material. Writing vendor self-test multiples as general conclusions, or treating unpublished specifications as confirmed, both erase the boundaries of the source material.

How: The order of questions before procurement

First, establish what can be bought now: HPC SDK 26.11 is available and the next-generation Fortran compiler is in public beta, while the rack and Rubin HPC are not in a procurable state. Second, ask about rack power and distribution: the official material only says the NVL72 chassis, power distribution and cooling components are reused and gives no system power, so component reuse cannot be treated as confirmed power. Third, ask about GPU specifications: Rubin HPC memory capacity, FP64 throughput, power and system configuration are all unpublished and 2028 is only an expectation. Fourth, check the performance framing: 1.9 and 2.4 times come from the OpenFOAM motorBike Large self-test, the WRF figures of 1.6 and 2 times are a vendor projection, and the x86 comparison model is not named, so they cannot be written as general conclusions. Fifth, record the three dates separately in procurement documents: available, expected supply and roadmap each on their own line.

Boundaries: Unpublished specifications cannot be filled in

Several boundaries need to be kept. Rack power is unpublished, and reusing NVL72 components does not mean the power is confirmed. Rubin HPC has no published memory, FP64, power or system configuration, so parameters cannot be inferred from the roadmap. Performance multiples carry a test scenario and an undisclosed comparison model and cannot be written as general conclusions. The forward-looking statement says products and timing may change and are not a commitment. Filling gaps with certain values is the most likely distortion in machine-room planning.

Conclusion: Record three dates separately

What can be confirmed from this set of announcements is: HPC SDK 26.11 is available now, the Vera CPU rack is expected from four suppliers in early 2027, and the Rubin HPC GPU is expected in 2028. DKRZ has selected Vera CPU for HLRE-5 with expected availability in early 2028, a user-side date. Rack power and key GPU specifications are unpublished, and performance multiples carry a scenario and an undisclosed comparison model. Recording available, expected and roadmap separately gives procurement documents a verifiable time and specification boundary.

Frequently asked questions

What can be bought now?

What is available now is HPC SDK 26.11 and the public beta of the next-generation Fortran compiler. The Vera CPU rack is expected from Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro starting in early 2027, and the Rubin HPC GPU is expected in 2028; neither is currently available for procurement.

What does the DKRZ selection mean?

The German climate computing centre DKRZ has selected Vera CPU for HLRE-5, built by Bull and expected to be available in early 2028, alongside GB200 and Quantum-2. This is a user-side selection and an expected date, showing that the platform has entered project planning; it does not mean it can be purchased or has been delivered now.

Can Rubin HPC be written into procurement specifications?

Not as a fixed value. The official blog from the same event gives only more than 50,000 CUDA cores per GPU, acceleration for sparse data, seventh-generation NVLink and an expected 2028 launch; it gives no memory capacity, FP64 throughput, power or system configuration, and the press release contains a forward-looking statement that products and timing may change and are not a commitment. It can be recorded as a roadmap item to watch, not as a confirmed specification.
