16 TOPS Developer Board: What to Check First

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

The 16 TOPS of the OrangePi O1 is the INT8 vendor peak of the TPU on the Sophgo BM1688, not measured model throughput. The vendor figures were relayed by CNX Software Chinese on 15 October 2026 and other technical media. On arrival, first check whether the Ubuntu image and documents are really available, then verify precision and model format, the storage exclusivity, cooling and power, and the network and camera interfaces, and finally measure with the target model.

The specification sheet

According to the relayed vendor figures, the OrangePi O1 measures 90×70mm, uses a Sophgo BM1688, and has an 8-core Arm Cortex-A53 at 1.65GHz plus a RISC-V C906 at 1.2GHz. TPU vendor peaks: 16 TOPS at INT8, 32 TOPS at INT4, 4 TFLOPS at FP16/BF16 and 0.5 TFLOPS at FP32. Memory is 8GB or 16GB LPDDR4/LPDDR4X. Storage is 16/32/64/128GB eMMC or 128/256Mbit SPI Flash, mutually exclusive. Interfaces include M.2 M-Key (PCIe 3.0 NVMe), a dedicated SATA 3.0, microSD, dual gigabit Ethernet (YT8531C PHY), Wi-Fi 6E/Bluetooth 5.3, M.2 B-Key (optional 4G/5G), HDMI 2.0 4K60, MIPI DSI and two MIPI CSI lanes, two USB3.0 and two USB2.0 ports, and 26-pin GPIO. Power is USB-C 20V/3.25A with a 12V fan header, the board weighs 76g and supports Ubuntu 22.04. The vendor says models such as Gemma-2B can be deployed locally, as vendor figures.

A peak is not throughput

The 16 TOPS belongs to the INT8 tier; the 32 TOPS at INT4, 4 TFLOPS at FP16/BF16 and 0.5 TFLOPS at FP32 are the same TPU at other precisions. A peak is a theoretical ceiling, while real throughput also depends on model format, memory bandwidth, cooling and the inference framework, conditions the specification sheet does not state. Reading 16 TOPS as the inferences per second of a model treats a theoretical ceiling as a measured result. The deployable models named by the vendor carry no throughput or test conditions and cannot replace measurement.

Check by use case

Anyone running local models should first check whether the Ubuntu 22.04 image and official documents are really available, then whether the model precision and format can be read by the framework. Anyone handling storage should first establish whether the board carries eMMC or SPI Flash, because the two are mutually exclusive and a wrong choice invalidates the boot plan. Anyone planning cooling and power should check the 12V fan header and USB-C 20V/3.25A supply. Anyone attaching peripherals should look at the dual gigabit ports, Wi-Fi 6E/Bluetooth 5.3, the 4G/5G option and the two MIPI CSI lanes. Different uses point to different items.

Relay, listing and verification

The vendor figures were relayed by CNX Software Chinese on 15 October 2026 and others, the time anchor for the source. The same outlet noted that the product page says an Ubuntu image already exists, but the Google Drive folder was still empty and the official documents had not been released; this is only a directional clue. The third-party retail listing date of 1 November 2026 and channel prices are likewise directional clues and stay out of the conclusion. The as-of date of 21 November 2026 is when the site checked the reporting; it is neither a launch date nor a listing date.

Specification, product page and channel

Dimensions, controller, CPU, TPU peaks, memory, storage and interfaces sit in the vendor specification sheet and were relayed by media, so they can be checked item by item. The Ubuntu image claim sits on the product page; CNX Software Chinese noted that the Google Drive folder was empty and the official documents had not been released, a pending state that stays out of the conclusion. The listing date and channel prices sit in third-party channels and also stay out. Turning a product-page line saying an image exists into an image that is actually available is the easiest way to mislead preparations before arrival.

The order of checks on arrival

First, check the image and documents: confirm whether the Ubuntu 22.04 image and official documents are available and record anything unavailable as an item to confirm. Second, verify precision and model format: confirm whether the target model uses INT8, INT4, FP16/BF16 or FP32 and whether its format can be read by the framework. Third, confirm the storage exclusivity: establish whether the board carries eMMC (16/32/64/128GB) or SPI Flash (128/256Mbit), since both cannot be installed together and the boot plan must follow the actual medium. Fourth, check cooling and power against the 12V fan header and USB-C 20V/3.25A. Fifth, check interfaces: the dual gigabit ports, Wi-Fi 6E/Bluetooth 5.3, the 4G/5G option and the two MIPI CSI lanes. Sixth, measure with the target model and keep the real throughput separate from the 16 TOPS peak.

Neither peak nor image status is fixed

Several boundaries need to be kept. 16 TOPS is the INT8 vendor peak, as are the 32 TOPS at INT4, 4 TFLOPS at FP16/BF16 and 0.5 TFLOPS at FP32: all are theoretical ceilings and none equals measured throughput. The deployable models named by the vendor carry no throughput or test conditions and cannot be written as verified performance. The product page says an Ubuntu image exists, but the Google Drive folder was empty and the official documents had not been released; this comes from a directional clue in CNX Software Chinese and can only be an item to confirm. The retail listing date of 1 November 2026 and channel prices stay out of the conclusion.

Verify availability before discussing peaks

The specification sheet of the OrangePi O1 is thorough, from the BM1688, the 8-core Cortex-A53 plus RISC-V C906 and the 16 TOPS INT8 TPU peak to 8GB/16GB memory and the full set of interfaces, all relayed by media. But 16 TOPS is an INT8 vendor peak, not measured throughput, and the deployable models are only vendor figures. On arrival, check the image and documents first, then precision, format, storage exclusivity and interfaces, and finally measure with the target model, keeping throughput separate from the peak.

Frequently asked questions

Can 16 TOPS be used directly as measured performance?

No. 16 TOPS is the INT8 vendor peak, INT4 is 32 TOPS, FP16/BF16 is 4 TFLOPS and FP32 is 0.5 TFLOPS, all theoretical ceilings. Real throughput depends on precision, format, memory, cooling and the framework, so it must be measured.

Can eMMC and SPI Flash be installed together?

No. The vendor figures state they are mutually exclusive: either 16/32/64/128GB eMMC or 128/256Mbit SPI Flash, one or the other. The boot plan must follow the medium actually on the board; the M.2 M-Key PCIe 3.0 NVMe, the dedicated SATA 3.0 and microSD are separate paths and do not replace this choice.

What should be done if the image and documents are missing?

CNX Software Chinese noted that the product page says an Ubuntu image already exists, but the corresponding Google Drive folder was still empty and the official documents had not been released; this is only a directional clue and stays out of the conclusion. Record availability as an item to confirm before the board arrives and check with the vendor or channel; if it is not available, do not plan as if everything were ready.
