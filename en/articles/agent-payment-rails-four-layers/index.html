x402, TAP, AP2 and MPP are not a four-way choice: they govern four different layers

Compute industry notes · GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon

GeekWaedon @GeekWaedon · November 2026

The biggest problem in public discussion of agent payments is that things from different layers get placed in the same comparison table, followed by the question “which one should we pick?”. The protocols mostly compose: one transaction can use a card network’s identity layer, another provider’s intent layer, a stablecoin settlement rail, and still be booked in the merchant’s own ledger.

Layer it before choosing

At the settlement layer: x402 was started by Coinbase in May 2025; Coinbase and Cloudflare announced foundation plans in September 2025; the Linux Foundation established the x402 Foundation on 2 April 2026 (publicly named members include Adyen, AWS, American Express, Cloudflare, Coinbase, Stripe, Google, Mastercard, Microsoft, Shopify and Visa; it became operational in July 2026). It revives HTTP 402: the server returns an amount, token, network and pay-to address; the client retries with proof of payment; a facilitator verifies and settles onchain. The protocol itself charges no fee.

The other layers make different claims. Visa’s Trusted Agent Protocol (October 2025) and Mastercard Agent Pay are card-network-side agent identification and authorisation. Google’s AP2, developed with more than 60 organisations, introduces signed mandates expressing user intent and permissions; in May 2026 the FIDO Alliance received the AP2 and Mastercard Verifiable Intent contributions. Stripe launched MPP in March 2026 for merchants already on its platform. Cloudflare’s August 2026 agentic commerce page lists TAP, Agent Pay, x402 and AP2 side by side as payment protocols a merchant can connect — which itself says they compose rather than compete. All of the above are vendor and foundation announcements, not third-party evaluations.

Three things that are easy to get wrong

Once the layers are separated, these are the decisions you still have to make yourself:

  • Caps before unit price: x402 settles in stablecoins, so settlement is final and there is no chargeback; card rails do have dispute handling, but a USD 0.001 transaction is not economically viable on a card network. Treating “the unit price is tiny” as “the risk is tiny” is a mistake — the real exposure is runaway call volume with no ceiling. Daily and monthly limits, category allowlists and a defined degradation path are yours to write; no protocol sets them for you.
  • Ask what adoption numbers actually count: third-party trackers and press reports have published figures as far apart as 35 million transactions / USD 10 million, 75.41 million / USD 24.24 million, and 100 million cumulative transactions. A Chainalysis account also cautions that much of the late-2025 growth was meme-coin activity rather than agents buying services. These are third-party statistics or press accounts, not foundation-audited numbers: direction only, never the denominator of a business plan.
  • Identity and compliance live elsewhere: x402 by design asks nothing about who is paying; KYC, fraud, refunds, invoicing and EU VAT are left to other layers. Cloudflare’s Monetization Gateway was still waitlist-only in 2026 with no published pricing, and AWS CloudFront’s generally available integration does not solve invoicing either. A mainland China entity first has to answer acquiring qualification and cross-border settlement — the same hurdle this site met when building its USD quote and compliance pages, and not a limitation of the protocols.

A rollout order that does not depend on the protocol

Write your own policy first — who may call, the price per call, daily and monthly caps, the idempotency key, what happens when a limit is hit — and only then pick a rail. Do not treat protocol defaults as commercial rules. For a content site the order should be metering and audit ledger first: caller, time, URL, price, verification result, delivery result, exceptions and compensation. Without a ledger, no rail can be reconciled; with one, changing rails is only an adapter swap.

Summary

When someone asked whether this site could take Stripe, the answer turned on qualification and settlement currency, not protocol selection. Agent payments are the same question one level up: first establish where the money comes from, who gets the invoice and how errors are unwound, then talk about 402. Reverse the order and you get a system that runs technically but cannot be closed financially.

Frequently asked questions

Are x402 and Visa Trusted Agent Protocol competitors?

They are not on the same layer. x402 handles per-request settlement by reusing HTTP 402 and stablecoins; TAP and Agent Pay are card-network-side agent identification and authorisation; AP2 supplies signed mandates expressing user intent and permissions; Stripe MPP targets merchants already on Stripe. Cloudflare’s official page lists them side by side as protocols a merchant can connect to.

Can published x402 transaction counts be quoted directly?

Be careful. Third-party trackers and press reports give widely differing volumes and amounts, and one Chainalysis account notes that much of the late-2025 surge was meme-coin activity rather than agents buying services. These are not foundation-audited figures; use them for direction, not as the denominator of a revenue forecast.

What should a content site build before accepting agent payments?

Its own call policy and audit ledger first: allowlist, unit price, daily and monthly caps, idempotency key, verification result, delivery result and compensation path. Once the ledger can reconcile requests, paid transactions and deliveries by day, switching rails is just swapping an adapter.
